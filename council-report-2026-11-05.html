Council report

5 advisors, 5 anonymous peer reviews, and a chairman's synthesis

Question: Is the 7-day AI-tutor plan a good way to prepare for the Amazon SDE Intern Online Assessment (OA), and for LeetCode in general? Or should you switch to NeetCode (the NeetCode 150 list and its videos) or something else?

Chairman's verdict

Where the council agrees

  • Don't switch to NeetCode this week. NeetCode 150 is a 3-month roadmap, and its videos are passive. All five advisors said that, after the OA, the NeetCode 150 list should become the problem queue through January. Use the videos only after you've attempted a problem yourself.
  • Practise under real test conditions. So far every practice rep has been in the tutor's friendly pages. You've never submitted on LeetCode, never hit hidden test cases, and never worked against a clock that can't be paused. Four of five advisors called this the biggest gap.
  • Confirm the exact deadline today, and do Amazon's demo today. The demo is the only real signal of what the format looks like.
  • The Work Style Assessment needs a proper slot of its own. The plan had squeezed it in at the end of Day 5.
  • Aim for partial credit, not perfection. A working brute force that passes some tests, plus a finished repo task, is a realistic goal for a beginner.

Where the council clashes

  • Is running code repeatedly a weakness or an asset? The Outsider and the Expansionist said the OA gives you Run and a debugger, so use them. The plan treats re-running as a crutch. Resolution: on the repo task, use the debugger on purpose. On the coding problem, trace the code by hand first, then run.
  • Take the OA on Day 5 or Day 6? First Principles said Day 5. Resolution: it depends on the real deadline. Keep at least 24 hours of buffer.
  • New topics on Day 4? The Executor kept sorting and greedy. The Outsider and First Principles said no new topics this close to the test. The chairman sides with no new topics.
  • How to prepare for the Work Style Assessment? The Expansionist and the Executor suggested writing STAR stories. Reviewers pointed out that the assessment is usually forced-choice questions, so stories matter less than reading the Leadership Principles and answering honestly and consistently. Stories are still worth writing later for interviews.
  • Should the repo drill be invented by the tutor? The Contrarian warned it would train for "the AI's guess" at the format. Resolution: do the demo first, then model the drill on what the demo shows.

Blind spots the council caught (from peer review)

  • Which language is the repo question in? If it isn't Python, the Day 3 practice is wrong. Check the invite and the demo.
  • No plan for the 2.5 hours. The council didn't decide what order to do the sections in, how many minutes each gets, or when to stop chasing the best solution and submit a brute force.
  • Syntax slips from memory. Without outside resources, mistakes like s.len(), else if and dict.get misuse cost points directly. Space-complexity work matters less this week, because the hidden tests check correctness.
  • The tutor is grading itself. Use something outside the tutor to measure progress: the demo, and LeetCode submissions.
  • Proctoring and integrity. No AI or tutor during the OA. Check the webcam and tab rules, the internet connection, and that you have a quiet 2.5-hour block.

The recommendation

Keep the tutor-led plan, but rebuild it around test conditions. Switch to the NeetCode 150 list after the OA.

Full advisor responses

The Contrarian

The fatal flaw is that the plan treats the OA as a learning milestone. It's really a test you are likely to fail whatever you do. A beginner who has solved about 8 Easy problems in an AI tutor's sandbox will not reliably solve an unseen Amazon Medium in 5 days, and NeetCode wouldn't change that either. So the real goal is to get as much partial credit as possible and avoid losing points through your own mistakes.

  1. No practice in the real environment. You've never submitted on LeetCode, never hit hidden test cases, never parsed input yourself, and never worked against a clock that can't be paused. Day 1 should include 2–3 timed LeetCode submissions.
  2. The tutor invents its own repo task on Day 3. That trains you for the AI's guess at the format. Amazon's demo is the only reliable signal, so do it on Day 1.
  3. The deadline is fuzzy. Confirm the cutoff today.
  4. The Work Style Assessment gets an afterthought slot, even though it may screen people out and is the cheapest part to prepare for.
  5. Your pace is a warning sign. Doing several lessons a day while still debugging by re-running suggests you're getting through lessons faster than you're retaining them.

Switching to NeetCode is wrong for the OA but right for January. After the OA, move to NeetCode 150 on real LeetCode, and keep the tutor for post-mortems.

The First Principles Thinker

The question assumes this is a choice between tools. It isn't. There are two separate problems on different clocks.

Problem 1 (7 days): One week can't turn a beginner into someone who reliably solves Mediums. What it can do is make sure more of your existing skill survives test conditions. The OA removes three crutches:

  • running the code repeatedly
  • looking up syntax
  • problems shaped like your lessons

The repo task is closer to normal software work than to puzzle-solving, and other candidates won't have practised it, so give it more than one day. The Work Style Assessment is scored and cheap to prepare for. Cut passive activities. Do every practice problem in a plain editor with a timer, no AI and no in-browser checker. Move the OA to Day 5 and confirm the deadline today.

Problem 2 (3 months): A list of problems covering the main patterns is the right tool, and NeetCode 150 is a fine one. But you haven't submitted anything on LeetCode yet. Lessons and videos both teach you to recognise a solution. Interviews test whether you can produce one cold. The unit of progress should be unseen problems solved without help, timed and talked through aloud.

The Expansionist
  1. The repo question is a hidden edge. OA prep material trains for the old format, so do 2–3 repo drills. In a VS Code-style IDE, your habit of running code to debug is an advantage, so learn the debugger deliberately.
  2. The Work Style Assessment is free points. Write 6–8 STAR stories. You can reuse them in the behavioural loop, at other companies, and in the January mock interviews.
  3. The tutor workspace is worth more than NeetCode, because it tracks your personal weak spots. After the OA, use the NeetCode list as the problem queue and the lessons as the engine for spaced repetition and mock interviews.
  4. Plan for the upside. If the OA goes well, the interview loop could come within 2–4 weeks. Start practising talking through problems aloud and doing mock interviews on Day 7.
The Outsider

The NeetCode question isn't the urgent one. Decide it after the OA.

  1. You haven't practised in the test format. Do at least two sessions on plain LeetCode or HackerRank, with a timer and no hints.
  2. "Runs code too often" is flagged as a weakness, but the OA rewards it. Don't train yourself out of tools you're given.
  3. Day 4 adds a new topic too late. Do timed unseen Easy problems on topics you've already covered instead.
  4. The Work Style Assessment is squeezed in. It can screen you out regardless of your code, so give it a real block of time.
  5. Facts are missing: the exact deadline, your daily hours, and whether it must be done in one sitting.
  6. Expect partial credit, not a perfect score. Write that goal down so you don't freeze.
The Executor

Keep the plan. NeetCode 150 is a 3-month roadmap and you have 7 days. Three things to fix:

  1. Day 1 is too small. Open the invite within the hour, confirm the real deadline, and do the demo today.
  2. You've never submitted on LeetCode. From Day 2, submit every problem on LeetCode with a 35–40 minute timer, taking problems from NeetCode's list. Watch a video only after a failed attempt. Aim for 2–3 unseen problems a day.
  3. Day 3 needs a concrete task. Clone a small Python repo with tests and run pytest. Break a function on purpose, then fix it using breakpoints.

Prepare the Leadership Principles in 45 minutes. After the OA, NeetCode 150 becomes the backbone, and the tutor drills your weak spots.
