Amazon's 16 Leadership Principles

Definitions from Amazon's Leadership Principles page. The "In Jassy's words" column comes from the talk transcripts you shared (CEO Andy Jassy explaining each principle). Taught in Lesson 16.

PrincipleThe core ideaIn Jassy's words
Customer ObsessionStart with the customer and work backwards. Watch competitors, but obsess over customers.Allowed honest book reviews when publishers objected. "Everything starts with the customer."
OwnershipThink long term, act for the whole company, and never say "that's not my job."Owners vs renters: "a renter will … nail the Christmas tree into the wood floor." If no one owns a problem, find an owner or own it yourself.
Invent and SimplifyExpect invention, always simplify, and take ideas from anywhere.Marketplace only worked after the "simplifying assumption" of putting sellers on the same product pages.
Are Right, A LotStrong judgment. Seek diverse views and try to disprove your own beliefs.It doesn't mean your idea has to win. "Get to the best possible answer … whoever's idea it is."
Learn and Be CuriousNever done learning. Explore new possibilities.Have a plan to change, then actually make the change. Talking about learning isn't enough.
Hire and Develop the BestRaise the bar with every hire, and coach others."Do not lower the bar," even under time pressure. Good managers name your growth areas.
Insist on the Highest StandardsRelentlessly high standards. Defects don't get passed down the line, and fixes stay fixed."Higher expectations lead to better results." Model what good looks like.
Think BigThinking small is a self-fulfilling prophecy. Set a bold direction and look around corners.Delivery went from days to two days, one day, the same day, then drones.
Bias for ActionSpeed matters. Most decisions are reversible. Take calculated risks.One-way doors get careful thought. Two-way doors ("most decisions") get made quickly, at team level.
FrugalityDo more with less. Constraints breed invention.S3 and EC2 were each built by 13-person teams. Don't get "numb to numbers."
Earn TrustListen, speak candidly, be self-critical, and benchmark against the best.It isn't being nice. It's being honest, challenging respectfully, delivering what you said, and owning mistakes out loud.
Dive DeepStay close to the details, audit often, and be sceptical when metrics and anecdotes differ."Follow the anecdotes." A 0.5% problem is millions of people.
Have Backbone; Disagree and CommitChallenge decisions respectfully, even when it's uncomfortable. Once a decision is made, commit wholly.10 ft vs 14 ft doesn't make 12 ft. Seek the truth, not a compromise. "I told you so" is useless.
Deliver ResultsFocus on key inputs, and deliver them on time and with quality, despite setbacks.Manage inputs, not outputs. "Launch … is not the finish line. It's the starting line."
Strive to be Earth's Best EmployerA safer, fairer and more productive workplace. Lead with empathy, and help people grow.An inspiring mission, at scale, with smart and motivated teammates.
Success and Scale Bring Broad ResponsibilityBe humble about second-order effects. Leave things better than you found them.Be a good member of your community: housing, food, education, disaster relief.

STAR, for interview stories

Situation (one or two sentences) · Task (what you had to do) · Action (what you did: say "I", not "we", and spend most of your time here) · Result (numbers if you have them, and what you learned).
